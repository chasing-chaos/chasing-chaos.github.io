Chasing Chaos
October 8, 2026

I Joined a Company That Promised to Revolutionize AI. Then Quit After One Week.

I once joined a company as a GenAI Product Manager.

During the interviews, they told me GenAI was a major strategic priority. The company had invested millions of dollars. High visibility. Groundbreaking innovation. Huge potential.

I was excited.

Then came my first day.

The internal website looked like something from the 1990s. Buttons everywhere. Text crammed into every corner. And somehow, in 2026, they were still printing out onboarding materials and handing them to new employees.

Then I discovered they had at least three internal chatbots in one org.

They looked almost identical. Did pretty much the same things. And nobody could clearly explain the differences.

I opened one and asked, “Who are you?”

It replied, “I’m Gemini.”

I asked a few more questions.

It crashed.

Apparently, one of these chatbots had been in development for six months. From what I could see, it was basically a wrapper around an existing LLM.

And guess what my first assignment was?

Increase chatbot adoption. 😅

How?

Fix the product? Improve the user experience? Figure out why nobody wanted to use it? Nope.

Create an AI Champion Network. Basically, appoint people in every department to encourage everyone else to use the chatbots.

The more time I spent there, the more I realized the company’s real expertise was PowerPoint.

Meetings all day. People doing actual work during meetings because there was barely any focus time. My manager was particularly proud of the PowerPoint decks he’d created.

Everyone was talking about AI transformation. Meanwhile, I was wondering whether PowerPoint was the only thing being transformed.

Less than a week in, I knew I’d made a mistake.

I told my manager the role wasn’t quite what I’d expected.

He was surprised and asked what I had been expecting. I considered sticking around a little longer, but he said that if I genuinely felt it wasn’t the right fit, I should tell him sooner rather than later.

Fair enough.

I figured it was better to leave before he spent weeks transferring knowledge to me.

So I quit. After one week.

But the story doesn’t end there.

Because apparently, the company forgot about my laptop.

I actually asked how to return it. Nobody gave me instructions.

Weeks later, HR suddenly contacted me with a very serious message.

“This has become a serious issue. You need to return your laptop immediately.”

I was confused.

“Sure. Can you send me a shipping box?”

Silence.

A few days later, another urgent message.

“You need to return the laptop ASAP!”

Me: “Where’s the box?”

Eventually, they sent one. I shipped the laptop back.

The whole process took longer than my actual employment.

I managed to quit in one week. It took the company much longer to figure out how to let my laptop quit.
